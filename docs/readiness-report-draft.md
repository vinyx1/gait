## The problem
*54 of 60 words*

Residents struggle to follow council decisions: reports run hundreds of pages, agendas change, and comment windows are easy to miss. We focused on three gaps: what is only recommended versus actually decided, whether residents can still comment, and how one decision affects people differently, such as wheelchair users, seniors using walkers, and shift workers.

## Your solution
*54 of 60 words*

TripLens explains Toronto council items in plain language: status, recommended versus decided wording, and open comment buttons, with AI rewrites software-checked against exact quotes. Residents also enter the bus or streetcar route they take and see decisions, works and stop access along it for a wheelchair user, senior or shift worker. Nothing is stored.

## What changes
*29 of 30 words*

Residents dig through PDFs and miss deadlines. TripLens shows recommended versus decided text with exact quotes, and who is affected differently, before a vote. It suits similar agenda systems.

## Adopting or scaling it
*46 words*

Each city needs an agenda export or feed, a GTFS transit feed (a national standard), open address data, and its own participation rules, since notice rules differ by province. Replace the illustrative rider limits with advice from local accessibility groups. Pilot with one clerk's office first.
